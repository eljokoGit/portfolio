Holiday rental agent

The management of my seaside rental, rebuilt in a week with Claude Code: bookings read from email, replies drafted for guests, a guest space in four languages, and scheduled tasks run by Hermes.

The project

A seaside flat rented on Airbnb, managed from Spain. The app follows each stay step by step, from booking to review, and replaces a spreadsheet and messages written by hand.

Every 15 minutes, an agent reads the booking emails. A plain reader extracts the figures; when a field is missing or the numbers do not add up, Claude takes over and proposes a fix to the reader. Guest messages always go through Claude, which drafts a reply. Nothing is sent without me.

Guests get a private space opened with their booking code, in French, English, Spanish and German. Its sections open as the stay goes on, with 53 activities, 10 restaurants and a daily events agenda.

What I did with AI

  • Code first, AI when in doubt

    The reader handles the routine; Claude steps in on an uncertain email or a guest message. Replayed on 230 real emails of the year: the 35 booking confirmations matched the Airbnb history exactly, dates and net amount.

  • Scheduled tasks in Hermes

    Hermes runs the jobs: mail every 15 minutes, the events agenda every night, a freshness check twice a week, a price review every Monday. Results land in one Discord channel. Claude stays the worker, launched only when there is work.

  • 225 conversations read

    Claude read 225 past exchanges with guests to write the frequently asked questions and the scheduled Airbnb messages, which I then validated one by one.

  • Prices from 79 neighbours

    A year of prices by period, built from 79 comparable listings, in a spreadsheet of 443 formulas, all checked. Every Monday, a job says whether the market has moved enough to redo the analysis.

  • Security decided upfront

    Each link is valid from booking to the day after departure, with no indexing, no analytics, and a lock after a few wrong attempts. The key box code never appears on the site.

What was hard

A place reopened by mistake

The problem
The freshness agent marked a closed nature spot as open again, on the word of a secondary website.
What I did
A rule written into the job: official source first, and a closed place only reopens on proof. Each closure is now stored with its dates.
The result
Fixed the same day. Guests never see a place that is closed during their stay.

A platform rule that changed the plan

The problem
The planned welcome relied on asking guests for their email. A new Airbnb rule forbids asking for any personal data from 20 November 2026.
What I did
Claude checked the policy before building further. The guest journey was redesigned around the private space and Airbnb's own scheduled messages.
The result
No more email request, and access never depends on the site: the arrival details are also in the Airbnb message three days before.
