A clear horizon for the eclipse

An agent computed the horizon at 1,961 points to find where the eclipse would stay visible, and I checked every recommendation on satellite view and Street View.

The project

On 12 August 2026, a solar eclipse peaked at 20:27 with the Sun very low in the west-northwest. From the Landes, outside the band of totality, the only question was where the Sun would stay visible above pines, relief and houses. I gave Claude the Sun's position at maximum (6.5° high, azimuth 284°), a 274° to 294° band as a safety margin, and an imposed architecture: read the open-source code first, reuse its physics, time a 20-point test before the full run.

Claude computed the horizon on a 200 m grid over a 5 km radius, kept the points reachable by a public road, then ranked them by confidence. When the best spot turned out to be a narrow roadside between two fences, the criterion changed: Claude ranked 813 public spaces from OpenStreetMap instead. I checked each recommendation on satellite view and Street View. Three errors were caught that way before the evening, and each one led to a new calculation.

The last adjustment came from me: the houses in the line of sight had no upper floor. With 5 m instead of the default 7 m, the final spot's margin rose from +3.20° to +4.52°.

What I did with AI

  • Read before writing

    Claude read the open-source repository first. Its per-site function made one network request per canopy pixel, far too slow for 2,000 points. Claude reused the repository's vectorised functions instead: elevation loaded once, only the 274° to 294° azimuths, numpy arrays.

  • Checked against the original

    On a test point, the new vectorised loop and the repository's reference function both return 0.418°. The timed 20-point test came first, then the full sweep ran in 4 seconds. Claude also read its own map and fixed an inverted axis label before sending it.

  • Constraints from open data

    A single OpenStreetMap query (7,490 elements) set aside points more than 100 m from a public road or in water, in a separate list rather than deleted. 37,554 buildings were added later, since neither the elevation nor the canopy data contains them.

  • Reading a Street View link

    When I sent Street View links, Claude read the camera heading written in the URL (297.68°, then 286.37°) and told me how far I was from the Sun's azimuth. It never saw the image, only its parameters.

What was hard

The buildings nobody modelled

The problem
The first recommendation, 1.1 km away with a +6.22° margin, sat in a housing estate. Neither the elevation data nor the canopy data contains buildings, as Claude had warned in its first answer.
What I did
I asked which point it trusted most. Claude fetched 37,554 buildings, computed their angle in the Sun's band, and scored each point by its worst margin within 250 m.
The result
Claude demoted its own first recommendations. A mapped viewpoint with the best terrain margin (+6.43°) was dropped too: one house 100 m away rose to 2.9°, right where the Sun would be.

The pin on the forest edge

The problem
On satellite view, I saw that a recommended picnic area fell in a wood. Claude had used the centre of the OpenStreetMap polygon, not the actual tables.
What I did
Claude recomputed at native 30 m resolution over 250 m around the pin: tree height and margin, cell by cell. When I then pointed at the houses nearby, it admitted this micro-map had left out the buildings, and re-ranked with at least 70 m from any building and at most 120 m from a road.
The result
The pin sat exactly on the boundary between meadow and wood: one cell south, the margin fell to −6°. The new ranking kept 19 cells, the best at +5.84° with the nearest house 143 m away.

The playground behind the trees

The problem
Claude recommended a village sports ground for its public facilities. On satellite view, I saw trees everywhere.
What I did
Claude traced the ray at azimuth 284° from the pin, then computed the margin at each mapped facility.
The result
A tree line cut the site in two: playground −7.46°, multi-sport court −3.25°, but football pitch +5.20°, tennis courts +5.38° and +5.50°, toilets +5.46°. The plan was kept, reorganised around the pitch.
